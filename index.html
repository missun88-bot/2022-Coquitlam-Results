Mayor

Stewart led at every voting place

Richard Stewart received 14,105 votes—68.2% of all ballots cast—and finished 8,738 ahead of Adel Gamar. Mark Mahovlich received 818 votes. Stewart placed first in all 27 voting-place entries, although the size of his lead varied.

Among regular voting places, Stewart’s share of all ballots cast ranged from 55.6% at Lord Baden-Powell to 75.6% at Walton.

Each ring compares one candidate’s votes with all ballots cast at the selected voting place.

Council

A familiar council—with two new faces

Craig Hodge topped the 22-candidate council field with 10,799 votes. Six incumbents were re-elected, while Matt Djonlic and Robert Mazzarolo won seats on council for the first time.

Behind Hodge and Teri Towner, the winning field was tightly packed: only 452 votes separated the candidates ranked third through eighth.

Looking ahead

Nearly one in four ballots was cast in advance

Advance-voting locations handled 5,062 ballots in 2022—24.5% of the citywide total. Looking ahead to 2026, Coquitlam voters can cast a ballot at any open location. The map shows locations scheduled for advance voting, General Voting Day or both.

Sources & notes

About the data

The results are based on the City of Coquitlam’s certified 2022 election results. Percentages in the rings represent candidate votes divided by all ballots cast at the selected voting place.

Council voters could select up to eight candidates. Council percentages therefore represent independent comparisons and should not be added together.

Voting-place results show where ballots were cast—not where voters lived. They should not be interpreted as neighbourhood voting patterns.

The 2026 locations and schedules are a dated planning snapshot. Confirm current information with the City of Coquitlam before voting.
